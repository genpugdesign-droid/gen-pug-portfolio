// EXAMPLE 01 — LEVI'S SIGNATURE RED

Levi's Boys Performance belt loop flasher, Canada

LEVI'S · SIGNATURE RED COLLECTION · REF P6972 · SP26 · EN / FR

01 / FINAL RESULT

A flasher that hangs from the belt loop of Levi's boys' 512 Slim Taper Performance jeans. The front pairs a lifestyle photo with a column of feature icons. A red band at the bottom carries the fit name in silver foil, and the rear holds the legal and origin copy. It comes in two sizes, one for 2T–7X and one for 8–20, and every word on it appears in both English and French.

02 / DESIGN EXECUTION

The layout puts the information in the order a shopper reads it: brand, then what the jeans do, then which fit they are. The left column holds the feature icons, each in a hexagon with a short English label and its French translation underneath. The photo fills the right column under the Levi's batwing. The 512 fit name and "Performance | Performant" sit in silver foil on a PMS 187 C band, so the fit is the first thing to catch the light on a crowded fixture.

Both sizes share one layout system, so the larger 8–20 version is the same design re-proportioned for a bigger card rather than a separate one.

03 / PRODUCTION DETAIL

The file sent to the factory is a 4-page mechanical. It has a title block on every page, front and rear mechanicals for each size, a feature-variable matrix, and a separate plate for the silver foil.

VARIABLES
16 feature-icon strips · each drawn for both sizes
PER-RUN FIELDS
Copyright year and country of origin · rules written into the file
Page 2 of 4. Live areas are boxed, and the notes on the rear tell the printer how to fill the year and country fields on every reprint.
Page 3. Sixteen feature combinations, A1 through H2, so the factory can pick the right strip for each style.
Page 4. The silver foil separated onto its own plate.

04 / MY CONTRIBUTION

  • Detailed the mechanicals on 07.31.25 and made the revisions on 08.18.25 (both credited to me in the title block)
  • Worked with the Haddad Brands Canada team so the English and French copy met Québec's Bill 96 French-language requirements
  • Built the 16-strip feature-variable system and drew every strip at both sizes
  • Labeled every dieline, live area, variable and per-run field so overseas factory teams and non-designers could read the file without having to ask
  • Split the silver foil onto its own plate and set the substrate, ink and non-printing specs

05 / OUTCOME

The result was one design system covering two sizes and sixteen feature combinations, in two languages, delivered as a single mechanical a factory can print from.
